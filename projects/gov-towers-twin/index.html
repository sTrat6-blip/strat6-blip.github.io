Does it match the reports?

Each design-day case below is recalculated in your browser and compared with future_scenarios.csv, from the Python model.

The reports quote a higher complex peak (3,048 kW) because they use the 30-day run, which adds day-to-day attendance and random noise. This twin runs the noise-free design day, so it reads about 3,002 kW.

Where the numbers come from

  • From CGI drawings: 2,000 kVA 13.8 kV/480Y/277 V dry-type transformer per pair at 8% Z, 3,040 A main breaker, 3,590 A bus, two 1,000 kW/1,250 kVA sets per pair, transfer within 10 s, 200 kVA UPS, AHF labelled 600 kVAR/950 A on a 400 A breaker.
  • Assumed in the load model: occupancy, desk density, plug loads, envelope, HVAC efficiency and floor area (scaled from the plans, ±15%). Every kW here rests on these.
  • Assumed in the supply study: outage behaviour, fuel curve, G$300/L diesel, Tariff GD rates, battery size, 60% first-step generator acceptance.

New in the twin, not in the reports

  • Indoor temperature is a simple one-zone estimate (0.05 kWh/m²·K of thermal mass). Use it to compare cases, not to predict comfort.
  • Cooling by wing splits the model's solar gain using the sun's position. The tower total is unchanged.
  • Site layout is estimated from one architectural render (south-west view), to about ±20 m. It is not labelled, so the near block being T1–T2 is an assumption, as are the far block's rear-facing position and the 15° rotation. Roads, parking and trees are indicative, and the substation and generator positions are not shown on any sheet received. Layout only affects the 3D view and the cooling split by wing, never the kW totals.
  • T1–T2 and T3–T4 differ here only by which floors you occupy. T3–T4 is drawn as a copy of T1–T2.
