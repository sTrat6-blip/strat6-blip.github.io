Method and limits

Yield. Monthly energy = kWp × peak sun hours × days × performance ratio. Temperature loss uses the mean cell temperature, ambient + (NOCT − 20)/800 × 750 W/m², against the module Pmax coefficient.

Sizing. Grid-tied and hybrid arrays meet the target solar share of annual load. Off-grid arrays meet the daily load in the worst month, including battery losses on the night share. The inverter is sized to peak demand, or to a DC/AC ratio near 1.2 when grid-tied.

Strings. Maximum length uses Voc at the record low ambient against the inverter DC limit and the MPPT ceiling. Minimum length uses Vmp at the hottest cell temperature against the MPPT floor. Vmp is scaled with the Voc coefficient as an approximation.

Energy balance. An average day is modelled each month. Daylight load is served first, surplus charges the battery, and stored energy serves the evening load. Anything left is exported or curtailed.

Economics. Savings are energy served by solar at the tariff, plus exports at the export credit, less O&M. The battery is replaced in year 12 at 60% of its first cost. LCOE spreads discounted costs over discounted solar energy.

Use. This is a feasibility tool. Check module and inverter datasheets, cable sizing, earthing and GPL interconnection requirements before you issue a design.
